# The brief (the user's request, 7 October 2026, verbatim)

Source of truth for the operating model: Priora playground definition.pdf (7 October 2026, in the Valcorv Drive and uploaded to this session).
Source of truth for the visual language: the folder "Priora Brand System — Working Direction — 2026-10-07" on the user's Desktop. That folder was not reachable from the cloud session that built v1; see the assumptions note.

---

Create a polished motion-graphics explainer for Priora with a final duration of exactly 1 minute 30 seconds.

The film should explain the complete Priora mechanism clearly and quickly: One Priora agent. A configurable site panel. Three decision rooms. Real work in the middle. A human making the economic decision.

This should be a motion-graphics film, not a presentation, a deck in motion, a product-interface demo or a sequence of static cards. Movement should do most of the explaining.

Structure

0 to 8 seconds, the real world. Begin with three simple black forms representing the worker, the physical site and the risk owner. They are people and places, not agents. Introduce the idea: "Real work in the middle. Agents around it."

8 to 22 seconds, everyday language becomes a case. The worker sends an ordinary voice message: "Hey, the bracket by the packing line has cracked again. We're going to weld it before the night shift." It should sound like something said naturally during work, not like a form being completed. As the worker speaks, let Priora quietly extract and organise the meaning: repair a cracked bracket; welding means hot work; packing-line location; before the night shift; applicable site and insurance conditions; missing evidence or information. Show the informal sentence transforming into a small, structured case. Priora may ask one simple follow-up question or request a photograph where information is missing. The visual idea is important: the worker describes the work in their own words; Priora turns those words into something the agent system can reason about.

22 to 38 seconds, the configurable site panel. Once Priora understands the activity, it summons only the specialists relevant to this particular site and job. For this example, show: Site Rules; Insurer Conditions; Fire; Risk Engineering; Evidence Checking. Make the panel visibly configurable rather than a permanent cast. Agents should enter as they become relevant. The Priora agent conducts the panel, distributes the structured case and gathers the responses. It is not a member of the specialist panel. Each specialist checks its own body of conditions. Show them exchanging focused signals, checking the evidence and returning scoped findings to Priora.

38 to 47 seconds, inside the conditions. The agents align around the case. If the activity remains inside the accepted conditions, the route opens, the work continues and the record is kept. No person is unnecessarily disturbed.

47 to 57 seconds, a deviation. Let one condition visibly fall out of alignment. The Priora agent gathers the findings into one structured decision packet and carries it to the human risk owner. Do not show Priora automatically stopping the work or making the decision. The agents check, ask and prepare. The human risk owner decides. A hard stop only exists if someone with the authority to create one has configured it.

57 to 78 seconds, three decision rooms. The deviation opens three clear economic decision rooms. Retain: keep the risk deliberately; make its exposure, authority, conditions and expiry explicit; Retain is never automatically selected by an agent and is never applied because nobody answered. Mitigate: compare possible safeguards by cost, time and reduction in exposure; select one and verify whether it brings the work back inside the accepted conditions; full mitigation returns the activity inside; partial mitigation sends the remaining risk onwards. Transfer: ask external capacity for eligibility, terms, required safeguards and price; mark this room clearly as SIMULATED; do not imply that an insurer already participates. Show that each room contains its own relevant agents and operates according to its own rules.

78 to 84 seconds, the rooms cooperate. Show the same structured case moving between the rooms. The risk owner may mitigate one part of the exposure, retain what remains or ask what transfer would cost before deciding. The Priora agent carries the case between the rooms. The human remains in control.

84 to 90 seconds, the complete system. Pull back to reveal the complete mechanism surrounding the real world: One Priora agent. A configurable site panel. Three decision rooms. Briefly suggest that hot work is the first use case and that the same mechanism can later be configured for other insured activities. Finish with: "Priora turns physical work into explicit risk decisions, while the work happens." Then show the canonical Priora wordmark.

Visual direction. Use the selected B+C direction: tactile moving glyphs operating inside clear decision chambers, with just enough individual character to recognise recurring agents. Use warm paper, black ink, subtle texture, precise geometry and restrained working colour drawn from the supplied brand assets. The real world should feel visually distinct from Priora's agent system. Build the film around these movements: summon; enter; inspect; request; return; align; deviate; assemble; escalate; route; compare; resolve. Lines should carry language, signals, evidence and cases through the system. Chambers should open, close, accept, reject and redirect. Motion trails may show where information has travelled, but they should remain controlled and purposeful. The everyday sentence should not simply become text boxes. Let its meaning separate, move and reorganise into the structured case before flowing to the relevant agents. Agent character should come from shape, aperture, one small visual cue and repeatable behaviour, not faces, limbs or expressions. The film should feel tactile, calm, premium, intelligent and economically serious.

Do not use: robots or humanoid AI; glowing neural networks; futuristic interfaces; generic dashboards; stock footage; literal Pac-Man characters or arcade ghosts; cute mascots; permit, checklist or documentation imagery; unnecessary 3D spectacle; conventional slide transitions. Do not turn this into a website or software walkthrough. Do not use an exploratory emblem as the logo. Use the current Priora wordmark as the only approved brand mark.

Sound and language. Motion should remain the primary explanation. Use a calm voiceover of approximately 150 to 165 words, leaving space for the visuals to breathe. On-screen language should be sparse: names, short actions and essential status labels only. Do not place the entire narration on screen. Use subtle physical sound design for speech fragments, arrivals, movement, evidence, alignment, deviation, routing and resolution. Avoid dramatic cinematic trailer music. The story must remain understandable without sound. "Playground" is the internal name for the complete system. In public-facing narration, call Retain, Mitigate and Transfer the decision rooms.

Maturity and accuracy. The complete system is a design proposal to be mapped and built. Do not imply that Retain or Mitigate are already deployed. Transfer is simulated first. Do not imply that an insurer already participates. The human risk owner always decides. Safeguard proposals are suggestions from a reviewed library unless a named risk engineer participates. Hot work is the first decided use case. Other insured activities are future possibilities. Do not include the revenue model, placeholder prices, internal responsibilities or open project questions in this 90-second film.

Deliverables: the finished 1920x1080 MP4; the editable source project; the final voiceover script; the timed storyboard; a version without narration; and a short assumptions note. Place everything in a new Desktop folder called "Priora Motion Explainer — v1".
